# v8.1.6a after-rewind corpus fix — 2026-05-28

Authoritative purpose: record the v8.1.6a training and evaluation run
that corrected two teacher-noise corpus defects in `kernel_forward`
(after-rewind canonical anchor style + next-event window outlier) and
its measured effect on the eval per-tool table.

Scope: documentation of completed work. No deployment, no adapter
install, no vLLM scale-up. The v8.1.6a adapter exists locally and in an
experiments archive; it has NOT been installed under
`/var/lib/operator-adapters/` and is NOT served by the vLLM endpoint.

## Hypothesis

The `kernel_forward:after-rewind:0291` regression v8.1.5 introduced
(over v8.1.3pa) was investigated and traced to a **deterministic corpus
defect**, not a model regression:

1. The `realistic.kernel_forward.after-rewind` family is the only
   forward family whose train rows are split between two
   `cursor_anchor` styles: about-style (11/21 train, 6/7 eval) and
   seq-style (10/21 train, 1/7 eval).
2. There is no input feature that disambiguates which style the model
   should emit. `last_tool`, `navigation_phase`, `known_refs`, `goal`
   structure are identical across both styles.
3. The seq-style values follow `seq:N = case_id + 1` arithmetic, which
   is information the model cannot extract from the input.
4. Both adapters (v8.1.3pa and v8.1.5) coin-flip on this family;
   v8.1.3pa happened to land about-style on eval row 0291, v8.1.5
   happened to land seq-style.

A separate eval row `kernel_forward:next-event:0112` had window=1
target while 17 of 18 train rows in that family used window=2, with no
input feature disambiguating either; the model correctly emits window=2
and the eval row is a teacher stochastic outlier.

v8.1.6a hypothesis: fix the two corpus defects, retrain on the
otherwise byte-identical v8.1.5-ask-pa base, and measure whether the
forward family converges to a clean per-tool score without disturbing
the rest of the profile.

## Per-row changes (13 rows: 11 train + 2 eval)

### After-rewind seq-style → about-style (10 train + 1 eval)

Rule: for each row with `task_family ==
realistic.kernel_forward.after-rewind` and
`action.arguments.cursor_anchor` starting with `seq:`, extract the
rewind anchor from the `goal` text via the regex
`rewound to (about:\S+?)(?:;|\s|$)`, assert it is present in
`user.visible_state.known_refs`, and replace `cursor_anchor` with that
anchor. `cursor_key` and `window` stay at `created` / `2`.

Rationale: in every one of the 11 affected rows the goal-mentioned
anchor was present in `known_refs`. The seq-style answer was a teacher
artifact (`seq:case+1`) not derivable from input; the about-style
answer is the rewind anchor the goal already names. This aligns the
family with its existing about-style examples.

| 2 | `kernel_forward:after-rewind:0425` (train) | 006 | `seq:7` → `about:…case-006:node:resolution:000` |

### Next-event window=1 → window=2 (1 train + 1 eval)

Rule: for each row with `task_family ==
realistic.kernel_forward.next-event` and `action.arguments.window == 1`,
set `window = 2`.

Rationale: the two affected rows share byte-identical goal text and
operator_state with the 17 train and 11 eval rows that have window=2;
no input feature disambiguates. Window=1 is a teacher stochastic
outlier (1/18 in train). The model correctly emits window=2 because
training proportion says so; normalising the two minority rows
eliminates the eval inconsistency.

| # | step_id | case | from → to |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | `kernel_forward:next-event:1653` (train) | 024 | `window=1` → `window=2` |
| 13 | `kernel_forward:next-event:0112` (eval) | 001 | `window=1` → `window=2` |

### Everything else: byte-perfect passthrough

The build script reads each base-corpus row, checks `task_family` and
arguments, and writes the **raw input line verbatim** unless the row
matches one of the two scopes above. This guarantees no accidental
re-serialisation drift on the 1787 unchanged train rows and 315
unchanged eval rows.

Other-family rows, ask rows, write_memory rows, ingest rows, and the
existing v8.1.5 `prepared_action` injections all pass through untouched.

### Limitations and known carryover

- The single `kernel_near` mismatch on
  `near:writer-pre-read-target-candidates:1890` is the same carryover
  defect present in v8.1.3pa, v8.1.4, and v8.1.5. It is a corpus-side
  defect in a different family and is out of scope for v8.1.6a.
- v8.1.6a has 9 `kernel_ingest` failures vs v8.1.5's 8. Eight of those
  9 are exact step_id carryovers from v8.1.5; one (`evidence-heavy:1870`)
  is new in v8.1.6a. All 9 are schema/grammar drift on long payloads,
  not budget truncation (the longest failure raw is 6063 chars = ~1500
  tokens, well under the 4096-token budget). This is the bucket the
  v8.1.7 evidence-heavy teacher augmentation went on to address (see
  `runs/2026-05-28-v8.1.7-evidence-heavy-teacher.md`).

## Production caveat

v8.1.6a inherits the v8.1.5 ask production caveat unchanged: the 32/32
`kernel_ask` requires `prepared_action` to be present in the operator
input at inference time. PR #50 plumbed the field through
`OperatorRequest`, but no production caller currently supplies it.
Without an upstream planner, deploying v8.1.6a would degrade live
`kernel_ask` below v8.1.3pa.

## Decision

- Keep v8.1.3pa as the deployed candidate.
- Do NOT install v8.1.6a under `/var/lib/operator-adapters/`. Do NOT
  load v8.1.6a into the vLLM endpoint.
- Use v8.1.6a only as a stepping stone to v8.1.7 (which builds on the
  v8.1.6a corpus and adapter family) and as a reference point for the
  corpus-fix methodology demonstrated here.
